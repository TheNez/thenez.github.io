The short version

FlightChat lets people traveling together, like families, friends or school groups, message each other when there's no internet, by connecting their iPhones directly. We don't run servers, we don't have accounts, and we can't see your messages, photos or anything else you do in the app. Everything stays on your phone and the phones in your group. The only exception is a report you choose to email us (see "Blocking and reporting" below).

What stays on your phone

FlightChat stores the following only on your iPhone, inside the app:

  • The name and group code you enter
  • A random ID created when you first set up the app, used to tell group members apart
  • Your messages, reactions and delivery receipts
  • Photos, videos, GIFs and files you send or receive

None of this is sent to us or to any third party.

What nearby phones can see

While FlightChat is open, your phone announces itself to other iPhones nearby that are running FlightChat, using Apple's peer-to-peer Wi-Fi and Bluetooth. That announcement includes:

  • The name you entered
  • Your random ID
  • A short fingerprint of your group code. The code itself is never broadcast.

Only phones with the same group code can connect to you. Other nearby FlightChat users may see your name in that announcement, but they can't connect, join your chats or read anything. When FlightChat is closed or in the background, your phone stops announcing itself.

How messages are shared

  • Group chat messages go to every connected phone with your group code.
  • One-on-one messages go only to that person.
  • When two phones in your group reconnect, each sends the other any messages it missed, but only ones that person was meant to receive.
  • Connections between phones are encrypted using Apple's built-in MultipeerConnectivity encryption.

Blocking and reporting

Blocking someone is handled entirely on your phone. Your phone stops sending them anything and hides their messages. They aren't told.

If you tap Report and Block, your email app opens with a report addressed to us at biomass-65-jumbo@icloud.com. It contains:

  • The reported person's name and random ID
  • Your name and random ID
  • The message you reported, if you reported one from a chat

Nothing is sent unless you tap Send in your email app, and you can edit the report first. We use reports only to look into abuse, and we delete them once they're resolved.

What we never collect

No analytics or advertising code runs in FlightChat. The app doesn't collect your location, contacts, phone number, email address, advertising identifier or device information, and it doesn't track you across other apps or websites.

If you've chosen to share analytics with app developers in your iPhone's settings, Apple may send us anonymous crash reports and usage statistics. We use these only to fix bugs. You can turn this off under Settings → Privacy & Security → Analytics & Improvements.

Backups

Your chat history is saved in the app's storage on your iPhone. If you back up your iPhone to iCloud or a computer, iOS includes that storage in the backup. Apple's privacy policy covers those backups.

Deleting your data

  • To erase all messages and media on your phone, tap the gear icon, then Clear Chat History.
  • Deleting the app removes everything it stored on your phone.
  • Messages you've sent remain on the phones of group members who received them until they delete them.

Children

FlightChat is made for groups of all ages, including families with children. We don't collect personal information from anyone, including children under 13. The group code controls who a child can talk to: only phones set up with the same code can connect.

Changes to this policy

If we change how FlightChat handles your information, we'll update this page and the date at the top. Any change that affects your data will also be described in the app's release notes.
