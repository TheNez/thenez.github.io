Getting started

Vortex Wind Tunnel has two pages, chosen at the top of the window:

  • Wind Tunnel: a live fluid simulation. Put an airfoil, a car, a train or any shape in the flow and watch the vortices form while lift and drag are measured.
  • Aircraft, Cars & Trains: typical vehicles and the forces on them: a wing's lift and induced drag, a race car's downforce, a train's resistance and top speed, and the vortices in their wakes.

Wind Tunnel

Air enters from the left at a steady speed. The sidebar sets up the simulation:

  • Reynolds number: low values (about 100) give smooth flow and a regular vortex street; thousands give turbulent, chaotic wakes.
  • Steps per frame speeds the simulation up; Grid trades detail for speed.
  • Shape: NACA airfoil, car, train, cylinder, flat plate, ellipse, freehand drawing or an imported model.
  • Show: smoke, vorticity (spin), speed or pressure. Smoke can be overlaid on the others.

Rotate by dragging. With the brush off, drag in the tunnel to turn the airfoil, plate, ellipse, car or imported model. Grab far from it for fine control.

Draw obstacles. Set the brush to Draw or Erase and drag in the tunnel to add or remove solid material.

Readouts. Lift Cl and drag Cd are measured on the body as the flow develops. For airfoils, the Airfoil theory tab compares them with inviscid theory.

Importing your own designs

Choose Shape → Imported, press ⌘I, or drop a file on the tunnel. Supported: STL, OBJ, PLY, USD/USDZ, Alembic and ASCII DXF. Save DWG files as DXF, and export FBX, STEP or IGES as STL.

  • Silhouette uses the model's outline seen from the side; Slice cuts through it, for example a car's centreline or a wing section.
  • Set which axis runs along the flow and which points up, flip it, and set its length, angle and whether it rests on the road.
  • Check the outline preview in the sidebar: that's exactly what the tunnel simulates.

Aircraft, Cars & Trains

Aircraft

Pick an aircraft from a high-wing trainer to a double-deck airliner, then change its wing, airfoil, speed, altitude, mass and bank angle. Trim finds the angle of attack where lift holds the weight. Drag the side view to change the angle of attack and the wake view to bank.

Cars

From a family sedan to an open-wheel race car: body coefficients, wings and speed. Drag the side view to pitch the car and watch the wing downforce change.

Trains

From a commuter train to a long-nose bullet train. Resistance is split into rolling, bogies, skin friction and the nose and tail. Drag the nose in the side view to lengthen it. The resistance chart shows where traction power runs out: the train's top speed.

Tips

  • See a vortex street: cylinder, Reynolds 150–300, Vorticity view.
  • See stall: airfoil at 15–20° and Reynolds about 1,000.
  • Save battery: pause the simulation with Space. A paused tunnel stops drawing until something changes.
  • Grainy flow or a viscosity warning? Lower the Reynolds number or raise the grid resolution.

The tunnel is 2D and runs at small Reynolds numbers, so treat its numbers as trends. The Disclaimer explains the limits.
