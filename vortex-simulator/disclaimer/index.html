What the wind tunnel simulates

The wind tunnel solves the flow with a lattice-Boltzmann method on your Mac's graphics processor. It shows how air really behaves, such as vortex shedding, separation and wakes, but its numbers differ from full-size reality in several ways:

  • It's 2D. Every shape is an endlessly long extrusion of its outline, so lift and drag coefficients describe a slice, not the 3D object. A 2D car or train has far more drag than a real one, and an imported 3D model is reduced to a silhouette or a slice.
  • Reynolds numbers are thousands of times smaller than for real vehicles (about 102–104 here against 106–108), so boundary layers, separation and drag differ.
  • The walls are close. The tunnel's floor and ceiling squeeze the flow and raise the forces.
  • Turbulence is modelled, not resolved, and very low viscosities push the solver to its limits. The app warns you when that happens.
  • Simplifications: wheels don't rotate, train wheels are left out, and the moving ground is a belt under the whole tunnel.

What the vehicle page calculates

  • Aircraft use lifting-line theory and an inviscid panel method for the airfoil. They assume attached, incompressible flow, so they ignore stall beyond a warning, compressibility at airliner speeds, the fuselage, the tail and engine effects.
  • Cars use typical published body coefficients plus lifting-line wings, without ground effect.
  • Trains use the Davis equation with an aerodynamic term built from simple parts, calibrated to one published train. Tunnels, wind, gradients, curves, acceleration and on-board power are left out.
  • Wake animations use point vortices in a plane behind the vehicle. They show the shape of the wake, not its real timing or strength.

Results are useful for seeing trends and comparing designs. They aren't predictions of real performance, safety margins, top speeds, energy use or stall behaviour.

Vehicle names and data

The presets are generic vehicle types, such as a narrow-body airliner, an open-wheel race car or a long-nose bullet train. Their dimensions, masses, speeds and powers are typical values for that kind of vehicle, taken from public sources and simplified for the models above. They don't describe any particular manufacturer's vehicle.

Imported models

The app turns an imported model into a 2D outline, and that conversion loses information. Check the preview in the sidebar, and don't treat results for an imported design as a substitute for testing it properly.
