What it does

Where Am I? shows your iPhone's live GPS readings: latitude and longitude, altitude above sea level, ground speed and heading, with a signal-strength antenna. It also records trips you can chart, replay and share, works on Apple Watch, CarPlay and with Siri, saves snapshots of your readings and can show your speed and altitude on the Lock Screen.

Getting a good signal

  • GPS works without internet or cell service, including in airplane mode.
  • Indoors, or in a plane, sit by a window. Metal and concrete block most of the signal.
  • The first fix can take a few minutes without internet, because your iPhone can't download its usual satellite assistance data.
  • The antenna shows how good the fix is. If it greys out, the app shows your last reading and its time until the signal returns.

Recording a trip

  • Tap Start Recording on the main screen. The trip keeps recording with the phone locked or the app in the background, and carries on if the app is closed.
  • Tap Stop to pause. Then Resume to carry on, Save to keep the trip, or Reset to throw it away.
  • Saved trips are in the Trips list (the map button, top left). Open one to see its distance, time, top and average speed, highest altitude, and total climb and descent.
  • Climb and descent come from your iPhone's barometer, which measures height changes more steadily than GPS. Allow Motion & Fitness access when the app asks; without it, they come from GPS altitude. Trips recorded before version 1.2, and trips from other apps, use GPS altitude.
  • Below the totals, charts show the trip's altitude and speed along the way. Tap a chart to see the values at that spot, then Play from Here to replay the trip from there.
  • The layers button at the top left of the map switches between standard, satellite and hybrid maps, and can color the route by speed or altitude.
  • Tap Play on a trip's map to watch it again: the map follows your car along the route and shows the time, speed, altitude and distance at each moment. It plays the whole trip in about a minute; tap the speed (for example 20×) to change it, drag the slider to jump around, and tap Whole Route to see all of it. Stops are skipped.
  • With iCloud Drive on, saved trips are kept in your iCloud account, so they come back if you delete and reinstall the app or set up a new iPhone with the same Apple Account. If you don't see them after reinstalling, give them a minute to download.
  • The route is shown on the map only when your iPhone is online. In airplane mode, record as usual and look at the map after you land.

Sharing trips

  • Open a saved trip, tap the share button, then Share Trip to send it as a Where Am I? trip file, by Messages, Mail, AirDrop or any other app. It shows up with the app's icon.
  • The trip file is the trip's GPX, compressed, so even a long trip is a small file, quick to send. To send plain GPX for another GPS app, tap Export as GPX instead.
  • When someone with Where Am I? opens the file, the trip is added to their Trips list with its map, charts and playback. Opening the same file twice doesn't add it twice.
  • GPX files from other GPS apps open too. To add a trip or GPX file from Files, tap Import Trips at the top left of the Trips list.
  • A shared trip shows where and when you went, so send it only to people you trust.

Siri and Shortcuts

  • Ask Siri "Where Am I readings" or "Get readings from Where Am I" for your position, altitude and speed, even with the app closed.
  • "How was my last trip in Where Am I" tells you your newest trip's distance, time and top speed.
  • "Save the trip in Where Am I" stops the trip and asks what to call it.
  • "Start a trip in Where Am I" and "Stop recording in Where Am I" start and stop recording. Starting a trip opens the app, because iOS starts GPS only while an app is open.
  • The same actions are in the Shortcuts app, so you can put them on the Action button or in Control Center.
  • Touch and hold the app icon for Start Trip and Trips.

Apple Watch

  • Where Am I? on your Watch shows your speed, altitude, position and heading. Swipe up for the trip.
  • While the iPhone app is open nearby (or recording, or on CarPlay), the Watch shows the iPhone's readings, marked From iPhone. On a plane, leave the phone still on the tray table by the window: it reads more steadily than a Watch on a moving wrist. Otherwise the Watch uses its own GPS, marked From Watch.
  • With the iPhone app running, the Watch's trip buttons record on the iPhone. Without it, the Watch records the trip itself, even with your wrist down, and sends it to the iPhone's Trips list when you save it and the two are in reach.
  • If you start a trip on the Watch and then open the iPhone app, the iPhone takes the trip over and carries on recording it as one trip, with its steadier GPS. It happens the next time the Watch app is on screen. The saved trip shows Recorded with, for example "Apple Watch 12:30, then iPhone 41:05".
  • The Watch uses the iPhone's units.

CarPlay

Connect your iPhone to CarPlay and open Where Am I on the car's screen to see speed, heading, altitude, position and the street and town you're in. While you record a trip, the street and town take the place of the coordinates, and the trip's distance, time and top speed show below. You can start, stop and save a trip from there too. Trips saved in the car are named after their start time; rename them on your iPhone.

To record every drive without touching anything, turn on Record trips automatically in the app's Settings, under CarPlay. A trip starts when Where Am I opens on the car's screen and is saved when your iPhone disconnects from the car. A trip shorter than 100 m isn't kept, and a trip you started yourself is left alone.

Saving battery

The screen uses most of the power. In the app's Settings, turn off Keep screen awake to let your iPhone lock, and turn off High-precision GPS for a small saving. Show on Lock Screen and recording a trip keep GPS running in the background, so turn the switch off and stop the trip when you don't need them.
